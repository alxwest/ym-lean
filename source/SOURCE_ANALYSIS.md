## Interpretive choices and scope

1. A Wightman presentation is a reasonable selected framework if shown at least as strong as the cited standard; requiring a second independent OS construction would add a method requirement. An OS presentation instead needs its corrected analytic/growth conditions.
2. Pure bosonic gauge-invariant observables justify bosonic locality. No gauge potential acting on a positive physical Hilbert space is demanded; forcing that could introduce a spurious obstruction.
3. General invariant tensors of the actual adjoint action, applied to curvature jets, are a natural group-sensitive classical observable language. Restriction to quadratic pairings can miss higher primitive invariants. Proving its algebraic presentation adequate is supporting equivalence work, not a YM existence proof.
4. Finite renormalization/mixing data may be witnesses, but equations linking them to a **fixed** perturbative construction are definitions. A proof author may choose an admissible presentation, not the prescribed singularities themselves.
5. Weak/distributional OPE remainders with spectator insertions with specified collision/contact restrictions are plausible; a norm-convergent operator OPE is an additional analytic demand unless supported by a fidelity argument. Purely pointwise expansions ignore coincidence singularities. One-loop leading behavior alone drops the perturbative-renormalization requirement.
6. §4 does not select a regulator or scheme. A chosen constructive presentation needs an equivalence/sufficiency argument before it becomes a universal obligation. Allowing quantified admissible schemes with fixed transformation laws is different from letting the proof author choose arbitrary predictions.
7. Footnote1 describes an expected filtered relationship and warns against naive bijection. It does not prescribe an exact filtered isomorphism as a separate mandatory theorem. Any stronger adopted condition must be justified as a presentation of correspondence rather than promoted from expectation to new requirement.
8. Connected compact simple Lie groups is the customary convention for the problem, but the source says compact simple gauge group and does not explicitly discuss disconnected extensions; document this convention rather than claim it is a literal source definition. Global forms with finite center must remain included.

## Nonrequirements and qualifications

No compulsory lattice, Wilson character, Haar regulator, preferred representation, confinement, isolated particle state, chiral-symmetry breaking, unique formalization, or different carrier per G. The extensions are explicitly separated in §5 pp6–7; methods in §6.1 p7 are alternatives. §6.5 p11 describes lattice limits as a possible route. Footnote2 p12 rejects weak compactness existence alone unless the limit's axioms and gap are established; this is consistent with witness data needing all fixed properties, not with requiring a specific construction.

## Exact central issue and next work

The outstanding issue is a mathematically fixed bridge from the pure G-Yang–Mills action to the perturbative predictions and the same nonperturbative fields. A general OPE expansion with existential coefficients can always be tuned and does not encode that bridge. Adding a positive beta coefficient captures only one leading feature, and imposing a Wilson-lattice limit silently selects a construction.

To complete this bridge, choose and document an admissible perturbative renormalization presentation; define its gauge/ghost combinatorics, graph amplitudes, local counterterms, composite insertion mixing and symmetry identities; derive fixed G-sensitive beta/anomalous/OPE coefficients; define precisely the RG-improved scaling and remainder conditions imposed on the actual quantum correlations; and establish scheme-independence or sufficiency of the chosen presentation. This is substantial missing definition/equivalence work, separable from the future existence proof. Until it is supplied, no complete exact-target admission is justified.
